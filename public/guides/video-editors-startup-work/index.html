Contributor guide

How video editors can find startup work

Startups may need product demos, founder videos, launch assets and short-form content—but the work, budget and expectations vary widely. A focused portfolio and a clear conversation help you identify a suitable opportunity without committing to vague promises.

What video work might a startup need?

The exact need depends on the product, audience and stage of the business. Examples include:

  • Short-form social videos: turning a clear idea or approved long-form recording into concise vertical edits.
  • Product walkthroughs: demonstrating a workflow, feature or use case with readable screen recordings and a clear story.
  • Founder-led content: editing interviews, explanations or behind-the-scenes material while preserving the founder's voice.
  • Launch and campaign assets: adapting approved footage and messaging into deliverables for a specific launch.
  • Customer stories: shaping genuine customer interviews, with permission and accurate representation.

These are examples, not a promise that every startup has a current opening. Check each opportunity's actual scope and availability before planning your time.

Build a portfolio for the work you want

Make it easy for a founder to judge fit quickly. Choose a small set of relevant examples instead of a long unfiltered reel. For each piece, say what you did—editing, sound cleanup, subtitles, motion graphics, story structure or repurposing—and whether the footage or final work can be shared publicly.

If possible, show different formats you can reliably deliver, such as talking-head edits, short-form clips, tutorials or product demos. Explain your contribution honestly. Do not claim performance metrics, client results or team responsibilities unless you can substantiate them.

Show founders how you work

A useful profile or introduction answers four questions: What kind of video do you edit best? What tools and workflow do you use? What turnaround and availability can you realistically offer? What information do you need from the founder to do the work well?

Instead of sending a generic message, connect your example to the startup's product or communication need. Offer a relevant observation or a clear next step, and respect the founder's time. Avoid mass messaging and never misrepresent the work you have done.

Agree on the scope before editing

“Edit our videos” is too broad to price or schedule accurately. Confirm the length and number of deliverables, aspect ratios, source-footage condition, subtitles, graphics, music licensing, number of revision rounds, deadline, file handoff and who provides approvals.

Discuss compensation before starting. A project fee or milestone arrangement should define what counts as complete. If a startup proposes equity instead of immediate payment, understand the amount, instrument, vesting, dilution, responsibilities and legal documentation. Equity is risky and is not the same as guaranteed cash. Consider qualified advice before agreeing.

Be cautious if a prospective client asks for substantial unpaid work, promises payment only after an uncertain funding event, or refuses to describe ownership and usage rights. A small, clearly agreed sample may be reasonable; open-ended free production is not.

Questions to ask before accepting a startup project

  • What audience and business outcome is this video meant to serve?
  • Who supplies footage, script, brand assets and approvals?
  • How many final versions and revision rounds are included?
  • What is the deadline, and who will give consolidated feedback?
  • What are the payment terms, licensing expectations and ownership rights?
  • Can the finished work be included in my portfolio, subject to any confidentiality terms?

Questions video editors ask

MornAI is designed to connect founders and skilled contributors, including video editors. Current opportunities depend on what founders publish and who is active; no particular job or payment is guaranteed.

Should I accept equity instead of a video editing fee?

That depends on your risk tolerance, the startup and the written terms. Understand that equity can become worth nothing, and do not treat informal promises as compensation. Consider qualified advice before agreeing.

What should I put first in my editing portfolio?

Lead with two or three examples that match the work you want, clearly state your contribution and include only claims you can verify.
